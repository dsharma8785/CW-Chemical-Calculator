Notes on this model
  • Dosing is deficit-based: today's ClO₂/NaOCl doses are sized to (target residual − today's measured residual), floored at zero — not a blind full shock dose regardless of what's already in the system. Measuring a residual already at/above target means zero additional dose of that chemical today.
  • Default (no chemical flagged): cost decides. Since cost scales linearly with ClO₂ usage, the cheapest choice is always one of two extremes — max ClO₂ (today's mechanical generator ceiling) or 0% ClO₂ (full NaOCl) — the model compares both and picks the cheaper.
  • Flagging a chemical short-supply switches to conservation mode: NaClO₂/HCl scarce throttles ClO₂ production down to whatever level makes that stock last exactly until the next delivery (using as much ClO₂ as sustainably possible). NaOCl/H₂SO₄ scarce forces ClO₂ up to the full mechanical ceiling to minimize NaOCl use. Both overrides apply irrespective of cost.
  • Flagged fields (amber) are placeholder assumptions pending real site/trial data.
  • H₂SO₄ = base (blowdown-driven, gated on today's CW pH — zero whenever pH is within/below the target band) + a causticity offset that scales with however much NaOCl is actually dosed today.
  • ORP and CW P-alkalinity are logged for reference/trending only.
